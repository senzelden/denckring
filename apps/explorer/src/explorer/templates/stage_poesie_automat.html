{# The board: six lines of six drums, each drum a window on a strip of flaps. Not a `.scene-split` — every other scene puts its figure in a 620px column with the panel beside it, and this one cannot: the widest line needs 134 characters of width and there is nothing like that left over beside a figure. The board takes the full stage and its panel sits underneath. **A departure from a literal board, and it is deliberate.** On the machine in Landsberg the drums stand in six true columns, every module the same width as the one above it. That board is 155 characters wide — the widest module of each column, summed — and 155 characters does not fit 1280px at any size worth recording. Sizing each drum to its own module's widest flap instead brings the widest line to 134 characters, which does fit. So the columns here are the machine's, not the eye's: a drum is as wide as its own ten flaps and no wider, and drums on different lines do not line up. Both figures are exact character counts over the shipped device (a test derives them); the rendered widths are in the task report. #}

{# The strip carries the module's ten flaps **three times**, and the drum rests on the middle turn. The drum is a window one cell tall and the strip is translated by whole cells, so a drum that ran past its last flap would have to jump nine cells back to reach the first — visibly, and in the wrong direction. Repeating the flaps removes the jump: cell *k* and cell *k* + 10 show the same words, so the position can be brought back onto the middle turn at any moment with the transition off and nothing on screen changes. Three turns rather than two, and the middle one rather than the first, because of the hand. A drum under a finger rests *between* two flaps, up to half a cell either way, so there has to be a real cell above the window and a real cell below it at every resting position. On two turns with the drum parked on the first, dragging down past the top flap showed the blank above the strip — measured, in a browser, before this was three. A clatter is at most nine steps and starts on the middle turn, so it can never run off the third. It is also what sizes the drum: the strip is as wide as its widest cell, so every drum comes out exactly as wide as its own longest flap without anything measuring a font. #}
