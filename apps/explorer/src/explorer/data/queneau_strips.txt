The paper discs are turning in the light. | Five rings are lining up a word tonight. | The workshop lamp is burning low and bright.
A word falls out of nothing but a ring. | The turning makes a word begin to sing. | There is no end to what a turn can bring.
The craftsman cut the letters down by sight. | No hand has ever set the discs quite right. | A million readings wait; not one is trite.
The rings go round and round, and start to swing. | A German word is such a curious thing. | The discs care nothing for the words they string.
The compositor will set it all again. | What comes off paper discs is seldom plain. | The sorts are scattered, gathered up again.
There is a word for this, and many more. | Such words were never spoken here before. | The letters lie in wait upon the floor.
Turn it again, and turn it once again. | The rings will spell a nonsense, in the main. | A thousand turns, and nothing much to gain.
The engine says no thing it said before. | That is the whole of it, and nothing more. | The reader is the one who keeps the score.
What any turning means is hard to tell. | The discs can spell, but only barely spell. | The word arrives, and no one rings a bell.
The workshop empties at the end of day. | There is a kind of sense in what they say. | The rings will turn tomorrow, come what may.
A German word can be a kind of spell. | The parts are dumb; together they can tell. | What language holds, no single ring can spell.
The letters go on turning either way. | And what is made is given clean away. | A hundred thousand poems in a day.
The discs keep neither reason, sense, nor time. | There is no craft in this, and yet no crime. | The whole of German turning, chime on chime.
And out of all that turning, now: a rhyme. | The rings have made a word, and it will rhyme. | So much from paper discs, and all in rhyme.
