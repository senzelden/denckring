    62. External evidence: public domain only, and every row says where it stands¶

    Context¶

    A checker and the examples its own author wrote agree by construction, so a green eval --all shows the code does what was meant, not that the form was read right. Only a golden case quoted from a published text is evidence about the reading. On 2026-10-12, 85 of 687 golden cases (12.4%) were externally sourced, and 77 of the 133 implemented rows had none at all; denckring-bench scores 127 rows, and 86 of those had none.

    Nothing recorded which of the 77 were work not yet done and which could never be done. s_plus_7, which the catalogue traces to Lescure in 1961, cannot have a public-domain exemplar; a sapphic stanza has hundreds. The corpus floor (EXTERNAL_FLOOR, 66) trailed the corpus by nineteen cases, it counted the whole corpus only, and nothing stopped a row losing its one external case while another row gained two.

    Decision¶

    Public domain only, from 2026-10-12. Golden cases added from 2026-10-12 quote public-domain text only and record the URL they were extracted from. They are extracted by script from a downloaded file, never retyped. The maintainer decided this on 2026-10-12. The public-domain cutoff, as set that day, is: published in 1930 or earlier (the US rule) and by an author, and translator, who died in 1955 or earlier (the EU's seventy years). A text whose author is unknown meets the death-year half through its publication date (amended 2026-10-13, below). Everywhere else, the code and docs say "the public-domain cutoff (ADR 0062)" rather than repeating these years, so that only this record needs correcting when they move. The policy is forward only: external cases shipped before it stay, including the post-cutoff ones listed under Consequences.

    What counts as an external near-miss. A published text its source presents as the form, or explicitly as a failed or impure instance of it: Hilton's verdict on "My Day Closed Is In Immortality" (ADR 0061 D4) is the model. A real poem of another form, used as a negative, is not external evidence. It shows nothing an invented text would not, and stays constructed if it is used at all.

    A gap registry. src/denckring/eval/fixtures/evidence-gaps.yaml maps each implemented row with no external case to {reason, note}. The reason is in_copyright (exemplars exist, none public domain), after_cutoff (the catalogue traces the form to a dated origin after the cutoff), unsupported_language (exemplars exist only in languages without a pack) or none_found, whose note names what was searched or says that no search has been made. A note states only what the catalogue row or the research batches record. A row whose catalogue attribution is reference cites a standard work ("attested in a standard work rather than traced to an origin", core/protocol.py), so the date is the reference's, not the form's. Such a row is none_found, not after_cutoff, unless its source names a dated origin as well: multiple_constraint cites only the 1981 Atlas, yet the corpus's own Bombaugh univocalic (1867) satisfies a composition in its golden file. s_plus_7 is the one reference row kept after_cutoff, because its source names Lescure, 1961. The registry was introduced with 21 after_cutoff and 56 none_found entries. The public-domain sourcing pass for the classical verse forms evidenced seven of them the same day (elegiac_couplet, heroic_couplet, iambic_pentameter, ottava_rima, sapphic_stanza, sestina, syllable_count), leaving 70: 21 after_cutoff and 49 none_found. Guards in tests/test_corpus_provenance.py hold that every implemented row is evidenced or listed, no row is both, every entry names an implemented row, and no none_found note is empty.

    Two floors. EXTERNAL_FLOOR rises to the measured 85, and EXTERNAL_SHARE_FLOOR (0.123, the measured share rounded down) stops constructed cases outpacing external ones unnoticed. Both were raised to the measured value again as public-domain cases landed, to 102 and 0.144 on 2026-10-12; the comments beside them in tests/test_corpus_provenance.py record each step. The share floor then fell, once and deliberately, to 0.141, when ADR 0063's German letter-rule cases landed, every one of them constructed; it was raised to 0.159 (118 of 739), with EXTERNAL_FLOOR at 118, on 2026-10-13 when the Bombaugh 1890 cases landed under the amendment below. On 2026-10-14 the public-domain cases of the pre-release pass raised them again, to 0.167 and EXTERNAL_FLOOR 125 (125 of 746). Later cases raised them further; with the Burmann and Rittler cases of the 2026-10-14 amendment below they stood at 0.174 and 131 (131 of 752) on 2026-10-15. tests/data/external-floor.json holds each row's external count as a ratchet: a row may gain cases and never lose one.

    Reported. Description.corpus is a CorpusShare: external_passing, external_failing, constructed, self_generated and gap, counted over every shipped case whatever the install can run. catalogue export carries it per row as corpus, null on a row with no checker. status and eval print N rows externally evidenced · M explained · K unexplained; K is 0 by the guard, and printed so that a broken guard shows.

    Consequences¶

    Grandfathered external cases. The policy does not reach back, and some external cases already shipped are post-cutoff publications. A scan of all 85 external source strings on 2026-10-12 found seven with a publication year after 1930:

    • lipogram gadsby-opening: Ernest Vincent Wright, Gadsby (1939);
    • palindrome mercer-panama: Leigh Mercer, Notes and Queries (1948);
    • cut_up reassembled-from-the-source: Gysin and Burroughs, Minutes to Go (1960);
    • eodermdrome the-word-itself, snowball borgmann-rhopalic and reverse_snowball borgmann-rhopalic-reversed: Borgmann, Language on Vacation (1965);
    • calculator_word hello: Quinion, World Wide Words (2009).

    Fifteen more give no year but are not inside the cutoff either. Two are calculator_word cases (illegible-with-nine-for-g, esel), whose practice the catalogue dates to 1970s pocket calculators. The others are web sources: Atlas Obscura's "Hair They Are" (paronomasia: curl-up-and-dye, grateful-head, jack-of-all-fades, i-kneaded-this; portmanteau: international-hairport, hairaphernalia), bedeutungonline.de (portmanteau: haarmonie, kammaeleon, sahaara, monhaarlisa) and tif.hair (portmanteau: imaginhair, atmosphair, diminutif). Two more quote pre-cutoff texts through post-cutoff editions: Kuhlmann's 1686 abecedarian in Beare's 1971 edition, and Hemeling's 1653 exchange as transcribed by Althaus in 1996. Few of the 85 record the URL their text was extracted from.

    Five rows count as externally evidenced only through grandfathered cases: cut_up, eodermdrome, snowball, reverse_snowball and calculator_word. There were six until 2026-10-13, when lipogram gained two public-domain cases from Bombaugh (1890). The per-row ratchet holds those cases in place, because removing one fails it. The maintainer ruled on 2026-10-14 that the cases stay and the five rows remain externally evidenced. The cost is recorded rather than removed: on those five rows "externally evidenced" rests on short quotations from publications after the cutoff, which is weaker than the policy now requires of any new case, and status prints them in the same count as rows evidenced by public-domain text. Only the cases' source strings, which name each publication and its year, and this paragraph tell them apart. The ratchet keeps them in place, so a row leaves this list only by gaining a public-domain case, as lipogram did.

    21 rows are after_cutoff and can never be evidenced while the policy stands: Perec, Queneau, Roubaud, Mathews, Lescure, Bénabou, Cage, Burroughs, Mac Low, Enzensberger, Zukofsky, Adair, Hecht and Pascal. Their checkers are as much one author's reading as any other row's, and no published text will test them. The registry says so; it does not fix it.

    The cutoff moves every 1 January, as another year's publications and another year's deaths enter the public domain. Nobody is obliged to revisit the registry when it does. An in_copyright or none_found entry can outlive its reason, and so can an after_cutoff entry once the cutoff passes the form's origin. The years stated above go stale the same way, and this ADR is the one place to correct them.

    A real poem of another form does not count, so a row whose only plausible negatives are real poems stays without an external near-miss.

    The none_found entries mean different things, and only the note tells them apart. There were 49 when this was written, 25 of them recording pending work: the batch 4 brief. Batch 4 reported on 2026-10-13. Three rows left the registry with Bombaugh cases it found (telestich, double_acrostic, pangrammatic_lipogram). ballade left on 2026-10-14 with four ballades from Gleeson White's anthology (1887), once the row took a metre, and petrarchan_sonnet the same day with Milton's Sonnet XVI, once the stress-scanning rows took the opt-in metrical licences of ADR 0065. Of the 44 that remain: - 18 record that the batch-4 reports this registry relies on did not search them. Three of those also record a search that failed in batch 3 (assonance_constraint, boustrophedon, paragram), and two a batch-3 find that was too incomplete to land (denckring, wechselsatz). - 4 record what batch 4 found: no English instance in Bombaugh's pp. 25–64 (bivocalic, letter_bank, serial_lipogram), and leads that each ban a single letter, so none would evidence a group (consonantal_lipogram). On 2026-10-14 one of them, the anonymous song without s that George Wakeman quotes in "Tormenting the Alphabet" (The Galaxy, 1866), landed as a lipogram case, extracted by script from archive.org's text layer and checked against the page image. Of the German R-lipograms, Burmann's 1788 edition did not land, because its scan has no text layer. The OCR of his 1796 edition and of Rittler's 1820 edition disagrees with the print often enough that correcting it is retyping, which this policy forbade; on 2026-10-14 the maintainer granted an exception for those two texts (amendment below), and one poem and one passage landed as lipogram cases. No scan of Rittler's 1815 edition was found, and Google Books and MDZ were not searched for it. Every one of them bans r alone, so consonantal_lipogram stays in the registry. The note records where each was sought. - 1 records a ruling on why the checker rejects every candidate found (alcaic_stanza): on 2026-10-14 the maintainer ruled that the row reads alcaics accentually, so Tennyson's quantitative "Milton" falls outside its definition. Nothing is pending, and the row still has no external case. - 3 record a search that failed, with nothing pending (haibun, ideenwuerfeln, pasigraphy). - 18 record that no search has been made and none is scheduled: the 16 Oulipo rows whose catalogue attribution is reference, plus sentence_length_constraint and single_sentence.

    The per-row ratchet is a file a person rewrites by hand when a row gains a case. If nobody does, the floor for that row stays at its old value and protects less than it could.

    Amendment, 2026-10-13: anonymous texts¶

    The cutoff asks when the author died, and an anonymous text has no author to ask about. Most of what C. C. Bombaugh's Gleanings for the Curious (enlarged ed., 1890) presents as instances of a form names nobody: the UNITE/UNTIE telestich (also a double acrostic), the Hatherton acrostic-telestich, all five univocalic verses (one of them a near-miss) and both e-lipograms (also pangrammatic). Read as written, the rule shut all of them out, although the book was printed in 1890 and its compiler died in 1906.

    Decision. The maintainer decided on 2026-10-13 that a text published in 1930 or earlier whose author is unknown (anonymous, printed in a dated compilation) meets the cutoff. The US rule is publication-based, and the EU term for an anonymous work runs seventy years from publication, not from a death. The case's source says "anonymous, printed " where a death year would go, and names the compilation's edition, year and compiler, whose own death year is still stated. A text the compilation attributes, such as Lord Holland's "Eve's Legend" in the same book, is judged by the original rule.

    Consequences. The rule admits texts whose author may have outlived the cutoff. A text printed anonymously near 1930 plausibly has an author who died after 1955: someone who wrote it at 25 in 1930 was 50 in 1955. The EU's publication-based term for an anonymous work also ends if the author becomes known, and the author's own term then applies, so a text admitted today can fall outside the cutoff when a scholar identifies its author. Nobody checks for either. The case that prompted the amendment is the low-risk end: for a text printed by 1890, the author must have written before 1890 and died after 1955. "Printed 1890" dates the compilation, not the text, which can be older; a text cannot have been published later than the book that prints it, so the compilation's date settles the publication half on its own. A compiler can misattribute, abridge or tidy a text, and with no author there is no other edition to check it against: the Bombaugh cases are transcribed from the Project Gutenberg text, and the page images were not checked. Read literally, the amendment covers an unknown author, not an unknown translator of a named author; the original rule names translators too, and no case so far has needed that question answered.

    Amendment, 2026-10-14: hand-corrected OCR of Burmann and Rittler¶

    Two German R-lipograms have a text layer that cannot be used as it stands. The SBB's OCR of Gottlob Wilhelm Burmann's Einige Gedichte ohne den Buchstaben R (Berlin: Decker, 1796) differs from the page images in 17 places in the 24 lines of "I. An Gott", and loses two words outright. The ÖNB's OCR of Franz Rittler's Die Zwillinge … Roman ohne R, 3rd ed. (Wien: Härter, 1820) needs 27 corrections in the four paragraphs of pp. 17–18 that land, and its errors on those two pages include four r's the print does not have, so a lipogram checker run on the raw OCR fails a text that is in fact without r. The rule above admits only text extracted by script, checked against the page image; correcting this much is retyping, and neither text could land.

    Decision. The maintainer granted an exception on 2026-10-14 for these two texts only. The case text is the script's extraction of the downloaded OCR with corrections made against the page image. The case's source lists every correction, as the OCR's reading and the print's, and states the normalisation applied to the Fraktur and Antiqua of the print: long s (ſ) to s, which is how the checker already reads it; ꝛ to r and a superscript e over a vowel to ä, ö or ü where they occur (neither occurs in the two landed passages, whose umlauts are printed as such); the print's double hyphen (=) to a hyphen, and dropped at a line break; spaces before punctuation closed up. Every other rule of this record applies: both texts are inside the cutoff (Burmann died in 1805; Rittler, born 1782, is given death dates from 1830 to "after 1837"), and each source names its edition, library, scan and OCR URLs and why the passage was chosen. Each correction is a literal pair applied by the extraction script, which fails if the OCR's reading does not occur exactly once on its page, so a correction cannot quietly miss. That script, like the other extraction scripts this record names, is not shipped with the repository: the safeguard ran when the cases were made, and a reader can check its result only against the correction list and the page images.

    Consequences. The two case texts are no longer pure script extractions. Every correction is a reading of the page image by whoever made it, and an error in one is a retyping error of exactly the kind the rule exists to prevent; the correction list makes each one checkable, but nothing checks them. The Rittler page images are a Google digitisation at modest resolution, and the Burmann passage is Antiqua with long s, so a misread letter is plausible in both. The normalisation also makes the text unlike the print: a reader comparing the case with the page sees s for ſ and joined hyphenations, and must take the source's word that nothing else moved. The exception does not extend to other texts, nor to other passages of these two books: the next text whose OCR needs correcting needs its own decision, and so does a further poem of Burmann's or a later chapter of Rittler's. Both cases ban r alone, so neither evidences consonantal_lipogram; they add German external evidence to lipogram, a row already externally evidenced.
