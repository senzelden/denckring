    63. Letter rules may claim German¶

    Partly supersedes ADR 0047, for one class of row.

    Context¶

    denckring-bench asked for German. Its suites draw only on rows that declare a language, because the suite here guarantees a satisfying golden case in every declared language and the bench takes that case as its proof that an instance can be met. A row that runs in German but does not declare it is, to the bench, a row with no German.

    ADR 0047 widens languages only where a row's own source names an attestation in that language. Over German that test returns zero: the four rows sourced to a German original already declared de. Measured on 2026-10-13 at the base of this decision, 126 of the 133 implemented rows run in German and 46 declare it, so 80 run unclaimed. The bench's ask cannot be met under ADR 0047, and ADR 0047 says so itself.

    Twenty of the 80 have a property the rest lack. Their checkers read letters and nothing else: no lexicon, no pronunciation, no metre. A lipogram in German is the same operation as a lipogram in English over a different text, and the German pack's alphabet, tokeniser and fold are what make it run. For these rows "is this a German form" and "is this rule checked in German" are close to the same question, and the second one can be answered by running it.

    Decision¶

    A row is a letter rule when its catalogue family is letter and its requires is a subset of {tokens, alphabet, fold_diacritics}. Every letter rule declares de. tests/test_letter_rules_claim_german.py computes the class from the catalogue rather than from a list, so a new letter row is held to it the day it lands.

    Measured on 2026-10-13, the criterion adds de to twenty rows: abecedarian, alphabetical_sentence, antigram, belle_absente, bivocalic, chronogram, consonantal_lipogram, double_acrostic, eodermdrome, homoteleuton, letter_bank, liponym, monoconsonantal, pangrammatic_lipogram, pangrammatic_window, paragram, serial_lipogram, supervocalic, tautonym, transposal. Twelve letter rules already declared it. The boundary case is kangaroo_word: family letter, but it requires lexicon.words, so whether it works in German depends on a German word list and not on letters alone. It stays outside, as do calculator_word, semordnilap, word_square and charade (lexicon), hemeling (phonemes) and prisoners_constraint (letter shapes), whatever they declare.

    For a letter rule, declaring de claims that the rule is orthographic and has been run in German. It does not claim that German literature attests the form. eodermdrome and liponym were named by English-language and French writers, and nothing here says a German writer ever practised either.

    The declaration carries what ADR 0047 made it carry. Fifteen of the twenty had no German name or definition, and each now has both. Fourteen had no satisfying German golden case and each now has one. All are constructed, and each was run through its checker, verdict and violation rules both, before it was committed. Four counterexamples sit beside them, three of which turn on an umlaut or ß.

    How German letters read under these rules is what Reading.normalization publishes for de: NFC composition, then case-folding with marks stripped, so ä, ö and ü read as a, o and u and ß reads as ss. With fold_diacritics=false the text is only lowercased, and since the German alphabet is the 26 plain letters, an unfolded ä counts as no letter of it. The cases are built to state which of the two they rely on. Every positive case whose row takes the switch holds with the fold on or off, so none of them passes only because an umlaut was read as its plain letter. Three of the counterexamples turn on the fold on purpose, and each is satisfied with the fold off: Wölfe breaks a line that must omit o, großen uses a forbidden s, and Äpfel sorts before Birnen. The fourth, liponym's, has no letters at issue.

    Consequences¶

    languages now means two things, and which one depends on the row. For letter rules it says the rule is checked in that language. For every other row it still says the form is a form in that language. A reader of describe().languages cannot tell which reading applies without also reading family and requires. That makes the field harder to read, and it was accepted in exchange for meeting the bench's ask.

    Rows outside the class stay as ADR 0047 left them. The bench also named anaphora, epistrophe, sator_square, sestina, pantoum, sentence_length_constraint, single_sentence and snowball_sentence. None of them is a letter rule: they are syntax, word, visual and form rows, and a criterion stretched to cover them would claim German forms on German capability, which is the claim ADR 0047 refused. Sixty rows still run in German without declaring it.

    The new German evidence is constructed, not external. The eighteen new cases prove the rules can be met under this checker's reading of German, which is the reading the bench grades against. They do not show that the forms are met in German writing. The corpus's external share fell from 14.5% (102 of 704) to 14.1% (102 of 722). ADR 0062's share floor in tests/test_corpus_provenance.py caught the fall and was lowered from 0.144 to 0.141 (raised to 0.159 the same day, when the Bombaugh 1890 cases landed; ADR 0062). The guard did its job. This decision chose to pay that cost.

    Six of the rows had German cases already, and most of them were never meant as German examples. They test ADR 0035's handling of letters with diacritics. bivocalic's is Bähö, monoconsonantal's is ßeiß with the fold off, pangrammatic_lipogram's and serial_lipogram's are bare alphabets, supervocalic's Rätsel bloß im Duft passes only because ä supplies its a, and abecedarian's umlaut-start exists to show that a start of ö reads as o. They satisfy the declaration's letter, and a German reader would not take them for German writing. The exception is abecedarian's second German case, which is external: Quirinus Kuhlmann's "Das güldne sterbens ABC" (1686). Among the twenty, it is the only external German case the checker accepts, and so the one place in the corpus where German literature attests a form this decision declares. chronogram also carries an external German case, the Tirol rifle-target lines Hilton prints, but the checker rejects it (wrong_total), so it attests the form in German without being an accepted example of it.

    Fifteen German names are new, and most of them are coinages. None is backed by a German source in this repository. They are listed by kind so that a reader who has a source can correct them:

    • Built from an established German term: Konsonantisches Lipogramm, Pangrammatisches Lipogramm (on Lipogramm), Doppelakrostichon (on Akrostichon). Each is a plain compound, not a term found in use.
    • Kept from the source language: Belle absente, Perec's French name, as English keeps it.
    • Descriptive coinages or calques of the English term: Alphabetischer Satz, Antigramm, Bivokalisch, Monokonsonantisch, Supervokalisch (after the catalogue's existing Univokalisch), Eodermdrom, Buchstabenbank, Liponym, Pangrammatisches Fenster, Anagrammpaar (for transposal) and Tautonym. Tautonym is a German word in zoological nomenclature, but its letter-play sense here is a calque.

    German inflects and compounds, and liponym matches word forms. With forbidden: Hund, Die Hunde schlafen and Der Hundeführer schläft both pass, because the checker compares whole tokens, ignoring case, and does not compare lemmas. English and French have the same gap with dogs and chiens, but German has more inflected forms and many more compounds, so it hits harder. A German reader may expect the plural to count. The row checks what its definition promises, a chosen word, and no more.
